Springer Nature 2026
Network-aware Path Planning for AMRs: A Generalizable Framework with Spatial-aware Adaptation
Om Kathalkar, Houssam Hajj Hassan, Ajay Kattepur, Georgios Bouloukakis
Industrial autonomous mobile robots increasingly depend on reliable wireless connectivity for real-time control and data streaming, yet existing path planning methods fail to account for the complex interplay between spatial geometries and material-dependent signal propagation. This paper presents a spatial-aware transformer framework for network quality prediction that explicitly encodes both geometric layouts and electromagnetic properties of building materials. Validation against the iV2I+ real-world dataset achieved R²=0.87 for SNR predictions. Evaluation across 8,840 test paths demonstrated SNR prediction RMSE of 2.31 dB (R²=0.891), a 19.5% improvement over state-of-the-art baselines. Zero-shot evaluation on mixed-material scenarios yielded only 10% performance degradation, eliminating facility-specific retraining.

IEEE ANTS 2025 2025
Network-Aware Path Planning for Autonomous Mobile Robots in Industrial Environments
Om Kathalkar, Houssam Hajj Hassan, Ajay Kattepur, Georgios Bouloukakis
Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs) in industrial environments require reliable wireless connectivity for coordination, control, and safety operations. This paper presents a framework for network-aware path planning incorporating wireless network quality metrics as path constraints. We validate our Sionna-based ray-tracing simulations against real-world measurements (R²=0.87 for SNR, 0.82 for throughput). Three novel planners were implemented: A* with network constraints, CVAE-based neural path planning, and GNN-based multi-path planning. CVAE achieved 95.2% constraint satisfaction; GraphMP showed 23% shorter planning times.

ICVGIP 2025 2025
AQIFormer: A Transformer-Based Multi-View Architecture for Cross-City Air Quality Classification
Om Kathalkar, Nitin Nilesh, Sachin Chaudhari, Anoop Namboodiri
We present AQIFormer, a novel transformer-based ensemble architecture addressing cross-city generalization in image-based air quality estimation. Through innovative dual-view integration, weather-aware attention mechanisms, and multi-task learning, AQIFormer uniquely combines front and rear traffic imagery with meteorological parameters. Evaluation on 26,678 synchronized image pairs demonstrates 89.96% accuracy — a 14.96% improvement over state-of-the-art. Cross-city generalization on an independent Nagpur dataset achieves 81.67% with only 8.29% performance degradation using few-shot adaptation.

ICVGIP 2024 Spotlight 2024
TRAQID — Traffic-Related Air Quality Image Dataset
Om Kathalkar, Nitin Nilesh, Sachin Chaudhari, Anoop Namboodiri
TRAQID is a novel dataset of 26,678 synchronized front and rear traffic images with co-located weather parameters, particulate matter (PM2.5, PM10) levels, and six-category AQI values. Collected over 70+ hours across Hyderabad and Secunderabad, India, it spans multiple seasons with diverse day/night imagery under unstructured traffic conditions. State-of-the-art methods trained on smaller datasets showed significantly degraded performance, establishing TRAQID as a challenging benchmark for image-based AQI estimation.

EnvSys 2023 2023
Protocol for Hunting PM2.5 Emission Hot Spots in Cities
Sara Spanddhana, Andrew Rebeiro-Hargrave, Om Kathalkar, Samu Varjonen, Sachin Chaudhari, Sasu Tarkoma
This paper proposes a protocol for using mobile search agents to identify PM2.5 emission hotspots in urban environments using IoT devices mounted on a mobile platform. Applied to Hyderabad, India, the protocol identifies short-range variability of PM2.5 using IoT sensing calibrated against a reference instrument. Random forest regression was the most effective calibration technique. Spatial variability at industrial settings and congested roads was identified, with temporal analysis based on image processing showing weak correlation between PM2.5 and vehicle count.

FiCloud 2022 2022
CV and IoT-based Remote Triggered Labs: Use Case of Conservation of Mechanical Energy
K. S. Viswanadh, Om Kathalkar, Nitin Nilesh, Sachin Chaudhari, Venkatesh Choppella
Remote Triggered Labs (RTL) enable students to conduct laboratory experiments virtually. This paper demonstrates computer vision-based RTL for the Conservation of Mechanical Energy experiment. A CV-based approach using a microprocessor, camera, and IR sensors estimates object velocity. Linear regression applied to the CV-based implementation achieved optimal MSE approximately 10× better than the IR-based approach, validating CV as a scalable solution for remote laboratory access.

Indian Patent 2025
System for Determining Air Quality Index (AQI) in Urban Environments
Om Kathalkar, Ansh Shah, Sachin Chaudhari, Anoop Namboodiri
A system for accurately determining Air Quality Index in urban environments with high spatial and temporal variability. Includes a mobile platform with multi-camera data acquisition (front, rear, 360°), PM2.5/PM10 sensors, noise sensors, and contextual sensors. An edge computing device processes data using deep learning-based feature extraction pipelines, cross-view alignment, and a transformer-based encoder with multi-head attention for six-category AQI classification.

Indian Patent 2024
System and Method for Determining Air Quality by Processing Environmental and Traffic-Related Visual Data
Om Kathalkar, Nitin Nilesh, Sachin Chaudhari, Anoop Namboodiri
Automated air quality determination using multimodal data fusion and a transformer-based deep learning architecture. Receives synchronized video streams from two cameras along with environmental sensor data. Images are processed using a frozen CNN to extract feature vectors, combined with sensor readings and contextual metadata, then fed into a custom transformer with positional encoding for six-category AQI classification.

US Patent 2024
System and Method for Implementing an Experiment Remotely and Determining an Output Using a Computer Vision Model
Om Kathalkar, Nitin Nilesh, Sachin Chaudhari, Venkatesh Choppella
A remote laboratory system that uses computer vision to evaluate and grade physics experiments conducted by students in remote settings. The system captures experimental setups via camera, applies computer vision models to determine physical outputs (e.g., velocity, trajectory), and compares results against expected values — enabling scalable, sensor-free remote laboratory access.
